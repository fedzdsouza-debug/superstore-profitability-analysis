05 — Where the hypothesis had to bend

Tables isn't a Central problem — it's an East problem. Central's problem is broader.

The pivot tables suggested Central's weak margin was a Tables/Bookcases story. Breaking Tables and Bookcases down by region shows something more specific: East's Tables loss (−₹11,025) is the single largest in the dataset — nearly triple Central's — and it comes with the highest average discount of any region/sub-category pair (37.4%). West's Tables orders, discounted at just 20% on average, are the only Tables line that's actually profitable. The pattern holds everywhere: more discount, more loss.

Central loses money on Furnishings, Tables, Appliances, Bookcases, Machines, Binders, and Supplies — a wide, shallow bleed rather than one bad product line. East's problem is narrower but deeper: one severely over-discounted Tables line dragging the whole region's Furniture average down.

06 — What this means

Recommendations

  1. Cap discounting on Tables and Bookcases at 20% Every region/sub-category pair above ~20% average discount loses money; every pair at or below it doesn't. This is a clean, testable threshold, not a judgment call.
  2. Investigate East's Tables discounting specifically A 37.4% average discount on Tables is an outlier even against other loss-making lines. Worth checking whether this is policy, a few large deals, or a sales-rep pattern before applying a blanket fix.
  3. Review Central's discount policy across categories, not just Furniture Central's problem is breadth — seven separate sub-categories bleeding money — which points to a regional pricing or approval practice rather than one bad product line.
